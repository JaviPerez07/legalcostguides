Missouri Lawyer Costs in 2026: What to Expect Before You Hire

Javi Pérez · Editor LinkedIn Last reviewed: June 2026 · Figures verified against the Missouri Revised Statutes (revisor.mo.gov), the Missouri Courts (courts.mo.gov), and The Missouri Bar
Legal Disclaimer: Information on this page is for general educational purposes and does not constitute legal advice. Attorney fees vary by case, experience, and location. Verify current fees directly with attorneys or official court sources before making decisions.

On This Page

Missouri legal fees run roughly $175 to $350 per hour in 2026 for everyday consumer, family, criminal, estate, real-estate, and small-business work, with a midpoint near $263. But the first dollar most people actually pay is a court cost, not an attorney fee — and in Missouri that cost is not a single statewide number. So this guide leads with the filing fees below, then moves to hourly rates and fee structures, because what you owe the clerk depends entirely on which circuit you file in.

Missouri's market is shaped by two anchor metros — Kansas City (shared with Kansas) and St. Louis — plus a wide rural belt where routine work is often cheaper but specialists are scarcer. The state's $5,000 small claims ceiling keeps minor disputes on a simplified track, and Missouri places no statewide cap on attorney contingency fees. This page pairs those rules with the real court costs and official Missouri resources so you can budget before you hire.

Court Costs and Filing Fees in Missouri

Here is the most important budgeting fact for Missouri, and one that surprises people who assume there is a single statewide rate: Missouri sets no single statewide flat small-claims filing fee. Under RSMo 482.345, the court costs and the required security deposit for a small claims case are fixed by Missouri Supreme Court rule and collected by each circuit, so the dollar amount varies by circuit and county. The claim ceiling, by contrast, is fixed statewide at $5,000 (RSMo 482.305). Treat the table below as a map of what to verify, not a quote.

E-filing surcharges and local administrative orders can change the total.

Why Filing Costs Differ by Circuit

Missouri runs its trial courts through 46 judicial circuits, each managed by a presiding judge and served by an elected circuit clerk. Small claims and most everyday civil and family matters are heard in the associate circuit division of whichever circuit covers the county. Because RSMo 482.345 lets court costs be set by Supreme Court rule and collected locally — alongside statutory surcharges for things like the law-enforcement training fund, the crime-victims fund, and sheriff service — the out-of-pocket cost for the same kind of filing can differ between, say, the 22nd Circuit (City of St. Louis) and a rural circuit a few counties away.

The practical takeaway: never assume the fee a friend paid in another county is what you will pay. Identify your circuit first, confirm the cost with that circuit clerk, and ask whether the court charges separately for service of process, certified copies, or a jury demand. If the cost is a hardship, ask the clerk about filing in forma pauperis (a fee waiver), which Missouri courts can grant to qualifying litigants.

Why Missouri Pricing Varies

Missouri has roughly 30,000 active attorneys per the Missouri Bar, with Kansas City (joint metro with Kansas) and St. Louis as the primary markets. Cost of living runs about 10% below the national average. Agriculture, transportation (the state's central highway position), and insurance drive routine practice. Major firms in St. Louis serve regional Fortune 500 clients (Anheuser-Busch InBev, Emerson, Express Scripts) at premium rates. Missouri's small claims limit of $5,000 keeps minor disputes accessible.

Lawyer Fee Structures in Missouri

Missouri lawyers generally bill one of four ways: hourly, flat fee, contingency, or a retainer drawn down as work is done. Hourly is the norm when scope can shift — contested divorce, civil litigation, business disputes, and criminal defense with multiple hearings. Flat fees suit repeatable tasks like a simple will, an uncontested dissolution petition, LLC formation documents, or a routine traffic or misdemeanor matter.

A contested-matter retainer in Missouri commonly starts around $1,750 to $5,260, with larger deposits for trial-heavy cases, emergency motions, or specialized business litigation. Contingency fees dominate plaintiff-side injury and some employment work, where the lawyer is paid from the recovery rather than billing monthly.

Unlike some states, Missouri sets no statewide cap on attorney contingency percentages. Instead, every fee is governed by the reasonableness standard of Missouri Supreme Court Rule 4-1.5, and ordinary personal-injury agreements commonly run 33% to 40%. Note one common point of confusion: RSMo 538.210 caps NONeconomic damages in medical-negligence cases (a base of $400,000, rising to $700,000 for catastrophic injury or wrongful death, adjusted annually) — but that limits a category of damages, not the lawyer's fee. Before signing any contingency agreement, ask whether case expenses come out before or after the attorney's percentage, whether the percentage rises if suit is filed, and whether a court must approve the fee (as it does for a minor's or wrongful-death claim).

Cost by Practice Area in Missouri

Personal injury: Most Missouri injury firms work on contingency, typically 33% to 40% of the recovery, and because the state sets no fee cap the exact percentage is whatever the written agreement says (subject to Rule 4-1.5 reasonableness). You may owe no hourly fee, but medical-record charges, expert costs, and deposition transcripts come out of the net, and serious cases can need tens of thousands in case investment first.

Divorce and family law: Missouri is an equitable-distribution state under RSMo 452.330, so marital property is divided fairly rather than automatically 50/50 — and the dispute over what is marital often drives the cost. An uncontested dissolution can be a flat fee plus the circuit filing cost; a contested one usually needs a retainer, with practical budgets from $2,625 to $15,750 or more once custody evaluations or business valuation enter.

Criminal defense: A Missouri misdemeanor may run from a few thousand dollars into the low five figures depending on charge level, record, and trial posture. Felony defense costs more because investigation, motions, expert review, and trial preparation expand the work. Ask whether the quoted fee covers trial or only a pretrial plea.

Business: Formation work can be a flat-fee package, while contract drafting, partner disputes, and commercial litigation bill hourly. A simple entity package may cost hundreds to a few thousand dollars, but a dispute can move quickly into a five-figure budget — especially in the St. Louis and Kansas City markets.

Estate planning: A simple will is usually the lowest-cost service here. Revocable trusts, tax-sensitive planning, blended-family provisions, special-needs planning, or beneficiary deed transfers raise the fee because the lawyer must coordinate documents and asset ownership.

How Missouri Compares to National Averages

At a roughly $263 midpoint, Missouri is moderately priced for routine legal work — below most coastal markets, helped by a cost of living about 10% under the national average. Read this as economics, not a quality ranking: it tracks local wages, overhead, attorney supply, court congestion, and the wide urban-rural split between the metros and rural circuits.

Neighbor comparisons help. See Illinois and Kansas for regional context, which is especially relevant in the shared Kansas City metro or if your matter involves a multi-state business. But a Missouri court matter generally needs a Missouri-licensed lawyer who knows the specific circuit, so treat out-of-state quotes as context only.

Cost of living is just one input. Lawyer density, legal-aid capacity, insurance-defense activity, and family-court volume all move the market, which is why comparing total scope beats comparing hourly rate alone.

Ways to Reduce Legal Costs in Missouri

Start organized: bring a timeline, key documents, deadlines, names, and a one-page summary to the first consultation so the lawyer spends time on strategy, not reconstruction. Ask for a staged plan — consult, document review, first filing, negotiation, and litigation only if it becomes necessary. Knowing your circuit's filing cost in advance (see above) also keeps the first invoice from holding a surprise.

For free or lower-cost help, try Legal Services of Missouri, the The Missouri Bar referral resources, and circuit self-help sections for self-represented filers. If you disagree with a bill, The Missouri Bar runs a Fee Dispute Resolution Program — voluntary, no cost, and client-initiated, offering facilitation, mediation, or binding arbitration. Limited-scope representation can also cut cost when you only need help with one filing or hearing.

Mediation and ADR lower spend when the dispute is mostly about money, parenting logistics, or contract terms rather than safety or high-risk rights. Use the mediation cost guide to compare ADR pricing against a litigation budget.
