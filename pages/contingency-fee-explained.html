Contingency Fee Explained

Javi Pérez · Editor LinkedIn Last reviewed: July 2026 · Sources: Clio Legal Trends, BLS OES, U.S. Courts fee schedules
Legal Disclaimer: This content is for informational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice. Always consult a licensed attorney for your specific situation.

On This Page

Contingency Fee Explained is structured so the client pays nothing unless the case wins. The 33%–40% standard, expense reimbursement rules, and ABA Model Rule 1.5 reasonableness requirements together shape what a fair agreement looks like. In practice, most contingency arrangements are defined less by any hourly benchmark than by the percentage the attorney takes and how case expenses are handled. What you ultimately pay depends on when the case resolves, whether costs are deducted before or after the fee is calculated, and how the agreement itself is written.

The practical budgeting question is not just “What does the lawyer charge?” It is also “What part of the matter is likely to get expensive?” For contingency fee explained, that can mean filing steps, records, experts, hearings, negotiations, discovery, or government fees that sit outside the lawyer's own bill. A contingency fee means the lawyer is paid a percentage of the recovery, not by the hour, but case costs and the percentage math still matter. Consumers should read whether litigation costs are deducted before or after the fee percentage is applied because that changes the final net recovery.

How Billing Usually Works

Different billing models exist because different legal problems carry different kinds of uncertainty. Routine, repeatable work is often cheaper to quote as a flat fee. Disputed matters with moving facts often require hourly billing or a replenishing retainer because the lawyer cannot predict the number of filings, calls, edits, or hearings at intake. A contingency fee means the lawyer is paid a percentage of the recovery, not by the hour, but case costs and the percentage math still matter.
