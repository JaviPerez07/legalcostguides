Personal Injury Lawyer Cost

Javi Pérez · Editor LinkedIn Last reviewed: July 2026 · Sources: Clio Legal Trends, BLS OES, U.S. Courts fee schedules
Legal Disclaimer: This content is for informational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice. Always consult a licensed attorney for your specific situation.

On This Page

Personal Injury Lawyer Cost follows a stepped contingency structure that rises as the case moves from pre-suit settlement through litigation and trial. The percentage matters less than how case expenses are reimbursed from the gross recovery. For context, hourly billing in comparable practice areas runs around $337 per hour in comparable work (Clio Legal Trends 2024), but almost no injury client pays that way: the contingency fee means you owe nothing up front, and what ultimately comes out of your settlement depends on the tier your case reaches and whether expenses are deducted before or after the fee is calculated.

The practical budgeting question is not just “What does the lawyer charge?” It is also “What part of the matter is likely to get expensive?” For personal injury lawyer cost, that can mean filing steps, records, experts, hearings, negotiations, discovery, or government fees that sit outside the lawyer's own bill. Contingency percentages commonly reported in the U.S. plaintiff market fall roughly between one third and 40% of the settlement or verdict, with case expenses reimbursed from the recovery. No single official source publishes a national contingency percentage; ABA Model Rule 1.5 requires only that a fee be reasonable and that contingency terms be set out in a signed writing. Many injured plaintiffs pay little or nothing up front, but defense-side or hourly consulting work can still require a retainer.

How Billing Usually Works

Different billing models exist because different legal problems carry different kinds of uncertainty. Routine, repeatable work is often cheaper to quote as a flat fee. Disputed matters with moving facts often require hourly billing or a replenishing retainer because the lawyer cannot predict the number of filings, calls, edits, or hearings at intake. Most plaintiff-side firms charge 33% to 40% of the settlement or verdict, with case expenses reimbursed from the recovery.

Contingency Fee Stages: How the Percentage Rises as Your Case Progresses

Most personal injury retainer agreements set a stepped contingency rate: the fee share increases at each procedural stage because attorney time and risk increase. Understanding these thresholds before signing prevents surprises if a settlement offer arrives after trial preparation begins.

Case stageTypical contingency rateWhat drives the differenceSource
Pre-suit settlement25%–33.33%No discovery, no motions, minimal attorney time; many insurers settle before complaint filingCommonly reported market practice; not a published statistic. ABA Model Rule 1.5 governs reasonableness and written fee agreements only
Post-filing, pre-trial33.33%–40%Complaint drafted, discovery exchanged, depositions taken; substantially more hours investedCommonly reported market practice. California Rules of Professional Conduct, Rule 1.5; Florida Bar Rule 4-1.5
During or after trial40%–45%Full trial preparation, expert fees, court time, post-verdict workABA Model Rules Comment [4] to Rule 1.5
After appeal40%–50% in some jurisdictionsAppellate briefing, record preparation, additional rounds of litigationState bar rules vary — confirm in writing before signing

Some states cap contingency fees by statute or court rule. California requires written disclosure of the fee agreement before work begins. Florida caps fees in medical malpractice matters. Always request a written retainer that specifies the exact percentage at each stage and whether case costs reduce the recovery before or after the contingency share is calculated. See ABA Model Rule 1.5 (Fees) for the national reasonableness standard.
