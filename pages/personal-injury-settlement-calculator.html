What the Math Is Actually Doing

This tool applies a structured severity multiplier to the hard-dollar parts of an injury case, then widens the result into a planning band. That approach is deliberately simple because the goal is not to mimic a carrier’s black box. It is to make the user better at comparing case scenarios and understanding why surgery, disability, or wage loss can change the economics quickly.

The estimate is most useful when paired with a case-specific guide such as trucking, motorcycle, or general personal injury cost pages. Those pages explain what the formula cannot see on its own, including venue, liability quality, comparative fault, insurance limits, and the strength of long-term medical proof.

When the Estimate Breaks Down

SituationWhy the calculator can understate or overstate valueWhat to do next
Weak liabilityEven a severe injury may settle low if liability is genuinely contestedRead the case-specific liability guide before relying on the number.
Low insurance limitsThe formula may generate value that available coverage cannot fundAsk counsel to analyze coverage early.
Catastrophic future careThe simple multiplier may still understate the role of life-care planningA firm may need economists and care planners to price the file correctly.
Strong punitive or corporate-liability factsThe formula may understate high-leverage casesUse it as a floor, not as the final ceiling.

That is why the number should be treated as a planning tool. It is a way to ask better questions, not a replacement for case valuation by counsel.
