Estate Planning Lawyer Cost

Javi Pérez · Editor LinkedIn Last reviewed: July 2026 · Sources: Clio Legal Trends, BLS OES, U.S. Courts fee schedules
Legal Disclaimer: This content is for informational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice. Always consult a licensed attorney for your specific situation.

On This Page

Estate Planning Lawyer Cost is mostly flat-fee work: simple wills run $300–$1,200, trust-based plans run $2,500–$5,000, and complex estate plans with business or asset-protection elements run higher. Probate work prices separately. Because most estate planning is predictable document work, attorneys typically quote a flat package price up front rather than metering time, with hourly rates reserved for unusual situations like contested matters or ongoing trust administration. What moves the price is the complexity of your estate — whether you need a will or a trust, how many assets require retitling, and whether tax planning or blended-family provisions enter the picture.

The practical budgeting question is not just “What does the lawyer charge?” It is also “What part of the matter is likely to get expensive?” For estate planning lawyer cost, that can mean filing steps, records, experts, hearings, negotiations, discovery, or government fees that sit outside the lawyer's own bill. Estate-planning lawyers often use flat fees for wills, powers of attorney, and revocable trusts, with custom hourly work for tax-heavy estates. Simple planning packages may not require a retainer, but high-net-worth families often face staged planning fees.

How Billing Usually Works

Different billing models exist because different legal problems carry different kinds of uncertainty. Routine, repeatable work is often cheaper to quote as a flat fee. Disputed matters with moving facts often require hourly billing or a replenishing retainer because the lawyer cannot predict the number of filings, calls, edits, or hearings at intake. Estate-planning lawyers often use flat fees for wills, powers of attorney, and revocable trusts, with custom hourly work for tax-heavy estates.
