Mediation Cost Guide

Legal Disclaimer: This content is for informational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice. Always consult a licensed attorney for your specific situation.

On This Page

Mediation Cost Guide depends on whether the program is private (hourly mediator at $150–$500/hr) or court-mandated (often free or low-fee). Hours required vary with case complexity and willingness to settle. Because most private mediators bill by the hour or half-day session, the real driver of your total bill is how many sessions it takes the parties to reach agreement — not the mediator's rate alone. A court-connected program with a nominal or waived fee can resolve the same dispute for a fraction of what a private mediator charges, which is why the venue you choose matters as much as the sticker price.

The practical budgeting question is not just “What does the lawyer charge?” It is also “What part of the matter is likely to get expensive?” For mediation cost guide, that can mean filing steps, records, experts, hearings, negotiations, discovery, or government fees that sit outside the lawyer's own bill. Mediation usually costs less than full litigation, but the savings depend on how prepared the parties are and whether lawyers attend. The cheapest mediation is often the one where both sides exchange records and realistic settlement positions before the session begins.

How Billing Usually Works

Different billing models exist because different legal problems carry different kinds of uncertainty. Routine, repeatable work is often cheaper to quote as a flat fee. Disputed matters with moving facts often require hourly billing or a replenishing retainer because the lawyer cannot predict the number of filings, calls, edits, or hearings at intake.
